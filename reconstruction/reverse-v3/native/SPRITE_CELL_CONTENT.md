# Which atlas do the item cells come from? The pixels decide

`original_item_types.tsv` claims `atlas_domain=TileMap:32x32` for the item domain, and
R17 corrected the geometry contract to match that claim. A claim is not evidence, so
this crops the recorded cells out of **both** candidate atlases and counts ink.
Generated by `tools/verify_sprite_cell_content.py`.

## Counts (current run)

| cells | atlas | rows | with ink | empty | out of bounds |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| formula domain (32x16) | HDTex/Items.png | 344 | **338** | 6 | 0 |
| image-id domain (32x32) | HDTex/TileMap.png | 82 | **82** | 0 | 0 |
| image-id domain (32x32) | HDTex/Items.png | 82 | 70 | 1 | **11** |

## What the pixels settle

- The **image-id domain lives in `TileMap.png`**: all 82 cells carry ink there, and
  none is out of bounds.
- Cropped from `Items.png` instead, the same 82 cells give 11 **out-of-bounds**
  rectangles - exactly the 11 the R16 geometry check reported before R17 re-attributed
  the domain to the 32x32 grid. R16's "violations", R17's re-attribution and this
  pixel test now agree: the numbers were never wrong, the atlas was.
- The formula domain's 344 cells land on ink in `Items.png` (338 of 344). The 6
  inkless ones are recorded as observed; item type 0 is a candidate (a null/air
  entry) but that is not verified here.

## Boundaries

- "Ink" = any non-zero alpha in the 64x64 cell. A cell whose art is entirely
  transparent-but-meaningful would read as empty.
- The test proves cells land on art in a given atlas; it does not prove the renderer
  reads that atlas at runtime (that is a call-site question).
- No device run, no draw.
